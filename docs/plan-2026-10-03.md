## Part 2: stash candidates (long-term holds)

**Your hypothesis:** some unclaimed players, mostly young, are about to be worth
more than their projection: more PP1 time than expected, a line with stars, or a
promotion when someone above them is hurt (Lundell after Barkov). The bot should
find and hold them.

All three triggers are one thing: **a change in deployment that the coach makes
before ice time and points show it.** A fourth source is young players
improving faster than the model's regression lets them.

### What the bot already does
- Keepers can be recommended today. Later points = whole-lineup projection over
  6 weeks x weeks left x 0.5, in win-pts, against the same add price. The
  shortlist includes the 3 best free agents per position by long-run value.
- Role is tracked from box scores: ice time and PP time, half-life 6 games.
  After 13 games the last 3 carry about 27% of the weight.
- DailyFaceoff's line chart (line, PP unit) is fetched every run but only used
  for "is he in the lineup". It never moves a projection.

### The probe (game logs, 2024-25 and 2025-26)
When a skater's role jumps over 3 games, how much of it lasts over the next 10?

| Jump over 3 games | n per season | Kept over the next 10 | Model's weight on those 3 games |
|---|---|---|---|
| Even-strength ice time +3 min/game | ~400 | **45-48%** | 27% |
| PP time +1.5 min/game | ~420 | 20-25% | 27% |

Fantasy points for the ice-time group: 3.85/game before, 4.2-4.6 during the
jump, 4.2 over the next 10. So:
- **Ice-time jumps are real, and the model is slow to believe them.** That
  under-projects exactly the players you describe, while their promotion lasts.
  This is a model fix (Part 1, item 2) and helps every decision, not just stashes.
- **"More PP time than expected" in box scores is mostly noise:** three-quarters
  of a PP-time jump is gone within 10 games. The coach's declared PP1 unit
  (DailyFaceoff) might predict better, but that's **untested**, and DFO history
  isn't archived, so it can only be tested after we log it.

### Gaps
1. **The earliest signal is unused:** the line chart, PP unit, linemates, and a
   teammate's injury pushing someone up the chart.
2. **Under-reaction to ice-time changes** (the probe).
3. **Option value.** A stash is a bet you can undo: if he breaks out you keep
   him, if not you drop him. Valuing him at his average projection undervalues
   high-uncertainty players (young, small samples). The bot values everyone at
   the average.
4. **Speed.** In a 16-team league a promoted player gets claimed within a day or
   two. The bot runs every 30 minutes and DFO updates its charts daily, so the
   edge is noticing first.

### Recommendation
**Not a separate rule beside the add price.** Two logics sharing 36 adds would
spend them twice and break the pacing. Instead:
- a **second candidate source** (role risers) that feeds the same evaluation,
- a **better valuation** for them (role-aware projection plus option value),
- the **same add price**, and a **watch line** when someone is close but not
  there yet.

### Phases, each gated on the one before

**P0: evidence (no behavior change)**
- *Done 2026-10-03, role blend:* no setting beats the current one. After an ice-time jump the
  model under-projects ~0.25 pts/game next week (~0.15 over 4 weeks) relative to everyone, but
  box-score ice time is too noisy to act on faster. The DFO line chart is the cleaner signal to test.
- *Done 2026-10-03, young players:* the opposite of a bonus was needed. The age curve's youth
  boost over-projected under-24s (+0.09 / +0.18 pts/game on every skater) and is removed; with
  it gone they're about unbiased (-0.09 / -0.01). Young stash candidates were overrated before.
- Role blend: separate half-lives for even-strength and PP time in
  `scripts/backtest.py`. Gate: MAE and pairwise better on 2025-26.
- Young players: are low-sample players (age 24 or under, under 100 NHL games)
  under-projected on average? Residuals by age and experience. Gate: a bias
  outside noise.
- Teammate-injury promotion, from game logs: when a team's top-6 center or top-4
  D misses games, who gains ice time, how much, and does it last while he's out?
  Gate: an effect in pts/game that's worth a roster spot.
- Start the DFO line archive (**D1**). It can't be backfilled, so DFO-based
  signals can be tested 6-8 weeks after it starts.

**P1: role-aware projection** (if P0 says so)
- The role blend from P0.
- A promotion caused by an injury lasts for the injured player's expected
  absence (the return curves we already fit), then reverts.

**P2: stash candidates in the plan**
- Source: free agents whose DFO line or PP unit is above what their ice time
  shows, those promoted by a teammate's injury, and young players in a top-6/PP1
  role. Plus the existing long-run shortlist.
- Value: later points from P1, plus option value. Simulate his true rate from
  the projection's own uncertainty (the Bayesian update already knows how many
  games of evidence it has). Value = expected best of keeping him or freeing the
  spot for streaming, minus the add and the streaming value the spot gives up
  while he's held (the hold logic exists).
- Message, when it clears the price: *"Stash: X (21) moved to line 1 and PP1
  with Barkov out (~3 weeks). Projection 2.1 -> 2.9/game while it lasts; +N
  win-pts later vs price M."* When it doesn't yet: *"Watch: X, clears the price
  if he stays on PP1 two more games."*

**P3: scorecard for stashes** over 4-6 weeks (the add scorecard looks at 14
days, too short for a hold).

### Honest expectations
- P0 and P1 almost certainly pay off: they improve every projection, and the
  ice-time part already shows an effect.
- P2's payoff is **unknown**. Genuine stash chances in a 16-team league may be a
  handful a season, and their value per hit depends on how long promotions last
  (P0 measures it). It could be worth a fraction of a win a season or several;
  there's no evidence either way yet.
- Cost: adds. With 6 kept for playoffs, about 30 for 23 weeks. Lopsided weeks
  like week 2 leave adds unspent, which is the natural budget for stashes.
